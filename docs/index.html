polysemanticity

The term trended due to a new essay from Nick Cammarata at Goodfire titled "Neural Networks Are Already Clean," which argues that previous evidence of polysemanticity was misinterpreted and that networks are more legible than previously thought.

Definition

Polysemanticity occurs when a single neuron or unit in a neural network activates in response to multiple, unrelated concepts. This phenomenon complicates mechanistic interpretability because it makes it difficult to assign a discrete, human-understandable meaning to individual components of a model. Understanding polysemanticity is critical for researchers attempting to 'decode' how AI models make decisions and for ensuring model safety.

Today in AI

Research into mechanistic interpretability is challenging long-held assumptions about how neural networks store information. Nick Cammarata of Goodfire argues that the perceived complexity of neurons and circuits in image and language models is not as arbitrary as previously believed. Specifically, evidence suggesting that networks pack concepts haphazardly through superposition may have been misread. Cammarata proposes a theory called "recursive generalization," suggesting that the internal mechanisms of these networks are more legible and structured than the field currently assumes, potentially simplifying the path toward better AI alignment.
