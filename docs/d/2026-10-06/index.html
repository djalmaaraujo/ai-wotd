Beam

Beam trended because Reflection AI publicly unveiled its flagship open-weight model today, publishing a detailed blog post and generating coverage in the Latent Space AI News digest. The launch reveals a 501B-parameter MoE architecture, massive RL infrastructure (10.5K GB300 GPUs, 100M rollouts), and positions the model as a U.S.-trained open alternative for coding and agentic tasks, driving concentrated discussion around the term.

Definition

Beam is Reflection AI's first open-weight large language model, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture with 501 billion total parameters and 23 billion active parameters per forward pass. It is designed for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads, combining large-scale pretraining on 23.8 trillion tokens with an extensive reinforcement learning post-training phase. As an open-weight release, Beam allows researchers and developers to download and run the model locally, contributing to the growing ecosystem of transparent, U.S.-trained foundation models.

Today in AI

Reflection AI launched Beam, a 501-billion-parameter sparse Mixture-of-Experts model with 23 billion active parameters, marking the company's first open-weight release after more than a year in stealth. The model targets coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads, drawing on 23.8 trillion pretraining tokens and a large-scale reinforcement learning run that produced over 100 million rollouts across 10,500 NVIDIA GB300 GPUs in four weeks. Reflection positions Beam alongside U.S.-trained open alternatives such as Nemotron and Inkling, though benchmarks place it behind newer Chinese models including GLM 5.3, Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8 Max, and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. The release highlights the continuing push to scale inference compute and RL-driven post-training as differentiators in the open-model ecosystem.
